# Rakupp answers deployment wishes; ecosystem gaps remain

> **Since this was written** (2026-11-07, at `1cb73808`): `Cro::HTTP::Client`
> turned out to work over https, HTTP/2 included, and the HTTPS,
> distribution-channel, language-server, GUI-showcase and Raku.js-recursion rows
> of the last table were corrected in `1b947272` and `0b14c4cf`. The lockfile
> question was settled in `3f8d8427`: LOCKFILE-PLAN is the plan. The debugger
> question is still open.

Rakupp already covers the Raku wishes with the longest and loudest record. Users have asked for a standalone executable since 2014, and Rakudo still cannot make one. Slow startup is the single most repeated complaint. Installation, Raku in the browser and an embedding API round out that group. The use cases rakupp does not yet cover sit one layer up: compatibility with particular ecosystem stacks, and developer tooling. Ranked by demand evidence, the largest uncovered or partly covered use cases are these. First, **data science and notebooks**: Antonov's Data::* modules, Math::Matrix and JavaScript::D3 fail on rakupp, and Rakudo passes some of them in the same harness. Second, **web applications above Cro's core**: Air, Red and Cro::WebApp, the "HARC" stack the community now promotes. Third, **text and log throughput against Perl 5**: rakupp's own benchmark puts Perl 1.8–3.3× ahead on splitting lines into fields. Fourth, **a step debugger**: none exists, and rakupp's plans contradict each other on whether to build one. Fifth, **reproducible, pinned installs**: there is no lockfile and no SBOM output. A second tier has moderate evidence: native GUI, Inline::Perl5/Python, LSP depth, modules in the browser, LLM tooling, distro packaging and cross-building. Mobile/Termux, JVM interop, Raku inside a database and multi-interpreter embedding have thin evidence. GPU/HPC, serverless, finance and microcontrollers have almost none. Nearly every wish below was voiced about Rakudo, so applying it to rakupp's users is an inference. The evidence base also lacks Reddit, direct Stack Overflow access and any Raku survey since 2020, so every count is a lower bound. Several sources that surfaced as "solutions" are the author's own project and are not counted as independent demand: raku.online, Raku.js, the Learn Raku app and the PyPI `rakulang` package.

The first two rows share a trait that makes them more tractable than they look. **The modules users want already exist, and several pass under Rakudo in the same harness.** That includes Math::Matrix, Data::Reshapers, Humming-Bird, Text::CSV and Inline::Python ([baseline](ecosweep/rakudo-baseline-2026-09-16.tsv)). So the gap is engine compatibility, which the ecosystem sweep can measure, not missing libraries. The authors are also already engaged. Anton Antonov, the main author of the data-science stack, publishes the RakuppLink Wolfram paclet. Fernando Correa de Oliveira, Red's author, reported issue #77 and runs rakupp's WASM build in his Raku Playground ([ADOPTIONS.md](../../../live/ADOPTIONS.md)). The sweep also overstates some failures. Cro::WebApp, Red and Jupyter::Kernel fail under Rakudo too, because the harness has no database servers or network. The sweep dates from 2026-09-29, before the v5.2.x fixes for Cro and DBIish. **A fresh whole-ecosystem sweep is the cheapest next measurement**, and it would show how much of rows 1 and 2 is still real.

The throughput row matters beyond its share of complaints. Rakudo users blame much of Raku's slowness on startup: "Most of the time you're seeing in the Raku code is the interpreter startup" ([HN 45532711](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45532711)). rakupp has removed that cost, so the remaining complaint is per-line work, where rakupp's own data still shows Perl ahead. Grammars and DSLs are the community's chosen beachhead: they won 6 of 12 votes in the 2025 poll, and #507 ranks them first ([problem-solving #507](https://github.com/Raku/problem-solving/issues/507)). That makes grammar speed weigh more than its mention count suggests. The concrete test is to add the exact tasks users posted as benchmark kernels: printing lines that contain a 15-character word, and pulling `request_id`/`fwd` fields from Heroku router logs.

The debugger and the lockfile share a different problem. Demand exists, but **rakupp's own planning documents disagree about whether to build them**. V5-IDEAS records the lockfile conflict itself: "the two documents disagree and one has to be right" ([V5-IDEAS.md](../../dev/plans/V5-IDEAS.md)). Neither can move until that policy question is settled.

GUI is where rakupp could move ahead of Rakudo rather than catch up. The domain research found **nothing in Rakudo's ecosystem that gives native look-and-feel on macOS or Windows**. Every Rakudo option binds GTK, Qt, IUP or libui, and in 2025 community advice was drifting toward web UIs and notebooks ([#raku-beginner](https://irclogs.raku.org/raku-beginner/2025-08-23.html)). The same research names a second, deployment-side GUI pain: shipping a desktop app means shipping Rakudo, the module tree and the toolkit's DLLs. GUI::Wings has Cocoa and Win32 backends, and `--exe` produces a single file. Released together and with Win32 actually run, they would address both pains.

Inline::Perl5 and Inline::Python matter more than their mention count. They are the standard answer to every "the library I need isn't there" complaint, which runs through the bioinformatics, data-science and CPAN-parity threads. So their absence widens every library gap in this report. The LSP row has its own test. #422's central complaint is that Rakudo's doc rendering and highlighting run `BEGIN` blocks and are unsafe on untrusted code ([#422](https://github.com/Raku/problem-solving/issues/422)). The inventory does not say whether `rakupp --lsp` avoids executing `BEGIN`, and that is the property to confirm before presenting it as the answer to #422. The LLM row lines up closely with the community's own strategy. A grammar-to-GBNF exporter would put rakupp directly on #507's third beachhead, for very little engine work.

Bioinformatics and scientific computing do not need their own rows. Their stated blockers are speed against Perl 5 on SAM parsing and Inline::Perl5 as the bridge to BioPerl ([wtwt5237](https://github.com/wtwt5237/raku-for-bioinformatics)), and those reduce to the throughput and Inline rows above. Bioinformatics requests also stopped appearing after about 2022. The embedding row is the one place where **rakupp's own adopters are better evidence than the web record**. Rakudo users barely asked for an interrupt API, but four of rakupp's own surfaces and at least one third-party adopter have had to work around its absence.

Researchers also looked for demand in several areas and found essentially none. The absence is weak evidence, but it is consistent. Searches for Raku with GPU, CUDA, OpenCL, HPC or MPI returned no Raku material at all. For **serverless**, there were exactly two signals. One HN commenter wanted to try Raku for "AWS Lambdas" ([HN 41914809](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41914809)). Another refused to install Raku for one library and wanted a distributable binary ([HN 38114430](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=38114430)). No Lambda runtime, Cloudflare Worker or Vercel request turned up. rakupp already has what a Lambda `bootstrap` needs: a static binary and 3 ms startup. The recipe is only an idea ([V5-IDEAS.md §12](../../dev/plans/V5-IDEAS.md)), so it would be cheap to write, but it should not be presented as answering demand. No **WASI** or edge request was found. **Finance** produced no domain library or request beyond marketing of exact rationals ([docs Rational](https://docs.raku.org/type/Rational)). **Microcontrollers** produced nothing either, so `--target=arduino` is supply ahead of demand. It is a typed subset whose guide says a program on real hardware is "Not yet tried" ([ARDUINO.md](../../guide/ARDUINO.md)). The `Arduino::WiFi` distribution on raku.land is the author's own upload, not a user signal ([raku.land](https://raku.land/?q=opencl)). Linters, formatters and coverage tools were also rarely or never requested.

## Rakupp's deployment features already answer the loudest demand

| Wish | Demand evidence | What answers it in rakupp | Residual |
|---|---|---|---|
| A standalone executable | **Strongest by duration.** Stack Overflow questions from 2014–2016; MoarVM #875 open since 2018 ([#875](https://github.com/MoarVM/MoarVM/issues/875)). These rows have one thing in common. They are the deployment and runtime problems that Rakudo maintainers publicly call structural. lizmat's advice was "if you want fast startup, don't use a scripting language" ([HN 42518463](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42518463)). jnthn named dynamically loaded extops as the blocker for standalone images ([MoarVM #875](https://github.com/MoarVM/MoarVM/issues/875)). Single-binary deployment, distro packaging and installation have been open requests in Rakudo for six to eight years ([MoarVM #875](https://github.com/MoarVM/MoarVM/issues/875); [user-experience issues](https://github.com/Raku/user-experience/issues)), which is exactly the opening an alternative implementation can take. rakupp has taken it, and the table gives a demand URL for each claim that rakupp's own pages can reuse. Covering a wish is not adoption, though. The June 2026 HN thread on the Raku Foundation had no commenter reporting production use, and its objections were social: switching cost, community size and naming ([HN 48541940](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48541940)).

## How far the demand evidence can be trusted

Almost every wish in this report was voiced about Rakudo, before rakupp existed or without reference to it. That rakupp's users want the same things is an inference. The evidence also has gaps of its own. **reddit.com could not be fetched or searched**, so r/rakulang, r/perl and r/bioinformatics threads, including the recurring "is Raku ready for production?" discussions, are missing. **Stack Overflow was reachable only through the kiwix mirror** (which returned HTTP 401 on direct fetch) and through search summaries, so question dates are estimated from IDs and vote counts are unknown. **There has been no Raku community survey since 2020**, and the 2018–2020 surveys never asked what is missing ([Raku/survey](https://github.com/Raku/survey)). The only recent poll had 12 votes from 760 views ([Rakudo Weekly 2025.51](http://rakuweekly.blog/2025/12/22/2025-51-exemplar-poll-results/)). Other sources were partly or wholly out of reach. Biostars and a Medium essay returned 403, a Lemmy thread was seen only as a snippet, Discord and Matrix were not reviewed, and the largest critical HN thread was read only to about 63%. raku.land's search renders client-side, so module-existence checks such as OpenCL or CUDA bindings failed. Every recurrence count above is therefore a **lower bound**, and "none found" means only that targeted searches came back empty. The visible voices are also concentrated among long-time community members (librasteve, lizmat, antononcube, klibertp recur across threads), which tilts the evidence toward insiders' priorities.

Several sources that the web research surfaced as current solutions are **the author's own project**, and none of them count as demand evidence: raku.online and Raku.js, the andrewshitov.com posts "Raku in a Browser" and "What is Raku++", the course.raku.org page on the rakupp compiler (the course itself is the author's grant-funded work), the Learn Raku iOS app, the PyPI `rakulang` package, the `Arduino::WiFi` upload and the GUI::Wings module. The third-party adoptions in `live/ADOPTIONS.md` are a different kind of evidence. They are real use by other people: Nigel Hamilton's binaries, Fernando Correa de Oliveira's Raku Playground, koans.app, Antonov's RakuppLink paclet, the TOTVS "xc" compiler. But they are recorded by the author, and they show adoption of existing features more than unmet demand. The exception is where they document workarounds, such as the stdout sentinels and the watchdogs.

## Conclusion

rakupp's coverage has an inverse shape to Rakudo's. Rakudo has broad ecosystem compatibility, and its unsolved problems are runtime and deployment problems its maintainers call structural. rakupp has solved most of those, and its strong-demand gaps are now mostly compatibility with modules that already exist and often already pass under Rakudo. Those gaps can be measured with the sweep and closed with engine work, unlike GUI toolkits, cryptography or a SciPy-scale stack, which are missing for every Raku engine. Two of them, the data-science stack and Red-based web, have authors who already use rakupp. The ecosystem re-sweep and Perl/Python text kernels built from users' own posted tasks would give the next ranking a firmer basis than any further web research.

The community's stated strategy names grammars and DSLs, web apps, and grammars that constrain LLM output, and it puts SBOM compliance on the table. Several low-cost rakupp items line up with it: grammar-speed kernels and a per-rule profiler, HARC-stack compatibility, a GBNF exporter, and SBOM output from `--exe`. Shipping them would place rakupp on the beachhead the community has already picked, with demand evidence already on record. By contrast, the cheapest items on rakupp's own idea lists (a Lambda recipe, a `FROM scratch` image, the Arduino target's next phases) have the least demand evidence behind them. They make good demonstrations, but they are not responses to anything users asked for.
